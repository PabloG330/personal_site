Most marketing channels are rented. The moment you stop feeding them budget, they stop feeding you traffic. SEO works differently. Done right, it's the closest thing in marketing to an owned, appreciating asset.

## Rented vs. owned

When you run paid ads, you're renting attention. Turn off the spend and the traffic evaporates the same day. That isn't a knock on paid. It's a fast, controllable, essential channel. It just never compounds.

Organic search compounds because every page you publish and every link you earn keeps working long after the work is done. One well-optimized page can bring in qualified traffic every month for years, at almost no marginal cost.

## What "done right" means

Compounding only happens when three things are in place:

- **Technical foundations.** A site search engines can crawl, understand, and trust.
- **Topical authority.** Real depth across a subject, not one-off posts scattered everywhere.
- **Content tied to intent.** Pages that match what people actually search *and* what your business sells.

> The brands that win at SEO treat it as infrastructure, not a campaign.

## The compounding curve

Here's the hard part. SEO is slow before it's fast. The first few months feel like nothing is happening. Then the curve bends, and traffic that took six months to build starts doubling on itself.

Survive the flat part of the curve and you end up with a channel that lowers your blended acquisition cost every single month.
